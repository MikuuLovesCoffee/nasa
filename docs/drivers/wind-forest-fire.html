Do wind and forest change fire?

1. Strong wind, by land type

Each dot is how often a month had big fire (≥30 detections in a 1° cell) when its strongest wind was calmer or windier than that place’s normal for the month, compared with all months of that land type. 1.0× means “no difference”. Lines show the 95% range.

2. Wind together with dry air

The same months split four ways: windy (strongest wind more than 1 standard deviation above normal) or not, and dry air (humidity more than 0.5 below normal) or not.

3. Forest loss and the next year’s fire

In cells with at least 10% tree cover: how often big fire happened in a year, by how much of the cell lost tree cover the year before (Hansen Global Forest Change). Cleared forest leaves dry debris and is often burned on purpose.

4. Does it improve the forecast?

The real test: add each group of inputs to the forecast model (which only sees last month’s data), choose on 2021–2022, then score on 2023–2025, years it never saw. “Unusual fires” are places and months that burn in fewer than 30% of years, where a forecast is most useful.

5. What the model leans on

Share of the model’s decisions (mean absolute SHAP value) per input group, in the model with every driver.

How much last month’s wind moves the predicted risk

Odds multiplier from the wind inputs alone (1.0× = no push), by land type and how windy the previous month was.

What this means

Data and method

  • Fire: NASA FIRMS MODIS C6.1 + VIIRS C2, 392.6 M detections, unified to VIIRS-equivalent counts (Ember Atlas harmonization), 1° monthly cells, 2002–2025.
  • Wind: NASA POWER (MERRA-2) wind at 10 m: monthly mean (WS10M) and strongest daily maximum (WS10M_MAX), 2000–2026, on a 2° grid.
  • Dryness and heat: NASA POWER humidity, rain, max temperature, root-zone soil moisture.
  • Forest: ESA WorldCover 2021 (10 m) tree share per cell; Hansen/UMD Global Forest Change v1.12 yearly tree-cover loss 2001–2024 (sampled every 300 m–1.2 km).
  • Greenness: NASA MODIS NDVI monthly (via NASA GIBS).

Anomalies are z-scores against each cell’s own calendar-month normal, so the regular fire season does not count as “wind effect”. Sections 1–3 describe association in the same month; they show what goes together, not proof of cause. Section 4 is a true forecast test with a time split. Cells are 1° (about 100 km), so local wind gusts and small fires are averaged out; the effects are regional.
